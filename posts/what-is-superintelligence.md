Super Intelligence (SI), often written as one word, superintelligence, is the name U.S. federal agencies now use for what most people still call artificial intelligence (AI). An executive order signed by President Donald Trump on September 29, 2026, titled "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," directs agencies to say "Super Intelligence" and "SI" instead of "artificial intelligence" and "AI."

The technology did not change. The vocabulary did. That matters for anyone who writes about, buys, or builds these systems, because for a while there will be two words for the same thing.

## What does the order actually change?

It changes how federal agencies talk. Agencies are directed to use the new terms in their non-statutory documents, reports, and websites, according to [Freshfields' summary of the order](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/trump-executive-order-mandates-shift-to-super-intelligence-102o403).

It does not rewrite anything already on the books. Regulations, contracts, grants, and historical documents don't have to be revised, and statutes are untouched for now. The order also borrows the existing statutory definition of AI (15 U.S.C. 9401(3)) for the time being instead of writing a new one, as the [IAPP points out](https://iapp.org/news/a/ai-by-any-other-name-white-house-directs-federal-agencies-to-enter-the-era-of-super-intelligence).

That "for now" is the part worth watching. The order gives the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology 60 days, until November 28, 2026, to send the President proposed legislative language for a permanent definition. Freshfields expects that a formal definition could affect future federal incentives, export rules, and compliance duties.

A separate industry accord was signed the same day. Freshfields describes it as voluntary: it adds no licensing or disclosure requirements and doesn't oblige the companies that signed it to use the new name.

## Is it "Super Intelligence" or "superintelligence"?

The order uses two words: "Super Intelligence," shortened to SI.

The one-word "superintelligence" is older. Philosopher Nick Bostrom popularized it for a hypothetical system that far exceeds human intelligence in nearly every domain, and labs such as Safe Superintelligence and Meta Superintelligence Labs use it in that future-facing sense.

So the word superintelligence now carries two meanings. One is the everyday tools federal agencies used to call AI: chat assistants, models, agents, automations. The other is the long-range idea of machines that outthink people. Context tells you which one someone means.

On this site, SI means the first one: the agents, models, and automations businesses use today. We spell it as one word because that is the established form, but you will see both spellings in the wild.

## Do businesses have to switch?

No. The order binds federal agencies, not private companies. You can keep saying AI on your website, in your contracts, and on sales calls.

There are still practical reasons to pay attention:

- **Federal work.** If you sell to agencies, expect requests, proposals, and reports to say SI. Match their wording in your responses.
- **Two vocabularies at once.** As the IAPP notes, statutes, state laws, contracts, and existing frameworks will keep saying AI while federal materials switch to SI. The current version of NIST's AI Risk Management Framework is one example. Internal policies may need to name both terms so nothing slips through a gap.
- **The coming definition.** November 28 is the deadline for the proposed definition to reach the President, not the day anything takes effect. Watch for it after that, because a legal definition of "Super Intelligence" could change which tools future rules cover.
- **Search.** People will search with both terms for a while. Pages that explain both are easier to find.

## How is the industry reacting?

Reaction is mixed, [Forbes reports](https://www.forbes.com/sites/siladityaray/2026/09/30/trump-signs-order-renaming-ai-to-super-intelligence--heres-what-it-says/). President Trump said he hopes the wider world adopts what he called the more accurate term. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang picked up the new language, calling data centers super intelligence factories, and Elon Musk reshared a White House official's clip of the signing gathering with the caption "Super Intelligence." Sen. Mark Warner of Virginia, vice chair of the Senate Intelligence Committee, said in a statement quoted by Forbes that renaming the technology does nothing about its real risks.

For most operators, the reaction matters less than the paperwork. Federal documents will change first. Contracts, laws, and everyday speech will catch up at their own pace, or not at all.
